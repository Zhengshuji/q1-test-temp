Affine Rank Minimization is ER-Complete
Angshul Majumdara
aIndraprastha Institute of Information Technology, Delhi, New Delhi, 110020, India
Abstract
We consider the decision problemAffine Rank Minimization, denotedARM(k):
given rational matricesA1,...,A q∈Q m×n and scalarsb1,...,b q∈Q , de-
cide whether there existsX∈R m×n such that⟨Aℓ,X⟩ = bℓ for allℓ∈ [q]
and rank(X) ≤k . We first prove membership: for every fixed k≥ 1,
ARM(k)∈∃R by giving an explicit existential encoding of the rank con-
straint via a constant-size factorization witness. We then prove∃R-hardness
by a polynomial-time many-one reduction fromETR to ARM(k)with the
input restricted to affine equalities plus a single global constraintrank(X)≤k .
The reduction compiles anETR formula into an arithmetic circuit in gate-
equality normal form and assigns each circuit quantity to a designated entry
of X; affine semantics (constants, copies, addition/negation) are enforced
directly by linear constraints, while multiplicative semantics are enforced by
constant-size rank-forcing gadgets whose soundness is certified by a fixed-rank
“gauge” submatrix that removes factorization ambiguity. We prove a com-
position lemma showing that gadgets can be embedded without unintended
interactions, yielding global soundness and completeness of the encoding and
preserving polynomial bounds on dimension and bit-length. Consequently,
ARM(k)is ∃R-complete (in particular,ARM(3)is ∃R-complete), establishing
that feasibility of purely affine constraints under a fixed constant rank bound
captures the full expressive power of real algebraic feasibility.
Keywords:Affine rank minimization; low-rank feasibility; existential theory
of the reals; ER-completeness; algebraic complexity; polynomial-time
reductions; arithmetic circuits; rank-constrained matrix completion

1. Introduction
1.1. Motivation
Low-rank structure is a unifying modelling principle across matrix com-
pletion, inverse problems, and system identification: one posits that the
unknown object lives on (or near) a low-dimensional algebraic variety, and
the observations impose linear constraints. In matrix completion and related
recovery problems, low rank underpins both statistical guarantees and algo-
rithmic design; convex surrogates such as nuclear-norm minimization are by
now standard, with sharp recovery results under incoherence and sampling
assumptions [1, 2, 3, 4, 5]. In control and system theory, rank minimization
formulations arise naturally via Hankel-structured models, realization theory,
and model reduction; early formulations and relaxations appear, for example,
in [6, 7, 8]. Across these domains, rank constraints are theonlysource of
nonlinearity, while the measurement model is affine.
This paper asks for theexactworst-case complexity of the corresponding
feasibility question in its cleanest decision form. Such a classification mat-
ters for two reasons. First, it explains why essentially all known practical
approaches must either relax (e.g., nuclear norm) or accept nonconvexity
(factorizations, alternating minimization) [6, 4]. Second, it pins down the
correct complexity class for algebraic feasibility over the reals, ruling out
“NP-hard but maybe still discrete” misunderstandings: the obstruction here
is fundamentally semi-algebraic.
1.2. Complexity background
The natural reference point for feasibility of polynomial constraints over
R is theexistential theory of the reals(ETR) and its associated class∃R.
Problemscompletefor ∃Rcaptureabroadspectrumofgeometricandalgebraic
feasibility questions; in particular,∃R sits between NP and PSPACE in the
standard bit model, and the PSPACE upper bound follows from quantifier-
elimination/decision procedures for the first-order theory of the reals [9]. The
Blum–Shub–Smale framework and the bit-model viewpoint clarify how real
computation and algebraic encodings interact [10], [11].
Our goal is to locate the following matrix-feasibility problem precisely
within this landscape: the input ispurely affine(linear equalities with rational
coefficients), and all nonlinearity is confined to asingle global rank constraint.
This is a particularly disciplined target format: it avoids presenting the
2
instance with explicit minors, determinant predicates, or any additional
polynomial constraints beyond “rank(X)≤k.”

rank(X)≤ 3. No explicit minor constraints, determinant constraints, or other
polynomial side conditions appear in the produced ARM instance.
1.4. Technical overview
At a high level, we reduce ETR toARM(3)by compiling arithmetic
reasoning into affine constraints ondesignated entriesof one matrix, and
using the global rank bound as the sole enforcement mechanism for nonlinear
semantics.
(i) ETR to arithmetic circuit constraints.A standard reduction converts
a general ETR formula into an equisatisfiable conjunction of polynomial
3
equalities, and then into a gate-by-gate constraint system for an arithmetic
circuit(oneequalitypergate). Circuitnormalformsofthiskindareubiquitous
in algebraic reductions and provide direct bit-size control [10, 9, 12].
(ii) Gate compilation to affine constraints on a single matrix.Each
gate value is assigned acarrierposition(ri,ci)and represented by the des-
ignated entryXri,ci. Linear relations (constants, copies, addition, negation)
become immediate affine equalities between carriers. The only genuinely
nonlinear gate is multiplication (and, when needed, inversion/nonzero), which
is enforced by constant-sizerank-forcing gadgetsbuilt from affine constraints
on a fresh set of auxiliary rows/columns.
(iii) Rank as the only nonlinearity: forcing via determinantal ob-
structions.The gadgets are designed so that any violation of the intended
identity (e.g.,z =xy) induces a4× 4submatrix of full rank, contradicting
rank(X)≤ 3. This is conceptually aligned with the geometry of determinantal
varieties (rank-bounded sets are Zariski-closed and cut out by minors) [13].
Conversely, when the identity holds, the gadget admits an explicit rank-3
completion.
(iv) Gauge fixing and well-defined decoding.Since rank-3factorizations
are non-unique up to an invertible change of basis, we pin a constant full-rank
3× 3submatrix (a “gauge block”) so that all decoded carrier values are
invariant across feasible rank-3realizations. This removes the only subtle
global ambiguity and lets the reduction compose gadgets without hidden
coupling.
1.5. Related work
Rank minimization and relaxations..Convex relaxations of rank (notably
the nuclear norm) form the backbone of modern low-rank recovery theory
[1, 2, 3, 4]. Nonconvex alternatives based on factorizations are also widely
used and often effective in practice, though they do not alter worst-case
feasibility complexity. In system identification and control, rank minimization
appears naturally via structured low-rank approximations and Hankel-based
formulations [6, 7, 8].
Hardness of rank problems in discrete settings..Several closely related rank
problems are known to be computationally hard in discrete models. For
example, variants of low-rank approximation and rank minimization over
affine spaces exhibit NP-hardness phenomena [14]. These results, however,
do not pin down theexactreal-feasibility complexity captured by∃R.
4
∃R-completeness as the right lens..A large body of work in computational
geometry and real algebraic complexity identifies∃Ras the correct class for
continuous feasibility problems, with reductions typically funnelling through
algebraic encodings and circuit normal forms [10, 9]. Our contribution is to
show that even under the highly restrictedaffine + one rank constraintinput
discipline,ARM(3)already captures the full expressive power of∃R.

2.2. Arithmetic circuits overR
Arithmetic circuits provide a compact representation of polynomial sys-
tems and a convenient normal form for∃R-reductions.
Definition 2.3(Arithmetic circuit).Anarithmetic circuitover R with input

following types:
1. input gates labeled by variablesxi;
2. constant gates labeled by rational constantsc∈Q;
3. binary gates labeled by+or×(fan-in2);
4. unary negation gates labeled by−(fan-in1).

Definition 2.4(Circuit size and bit-size).Thesizeof a circuit is the number
of gates. Thebit-sizeadditionally counts the total bit-length of all rational
constants (numerators and denominators in reduced form). Fan-in two is
without loss of generality: any higher fan-in addition or multiplication can be
expanded into a binary tree of gates with linear blow-up.

This section fixes the precise source normal form and target encoding

proved in later sections. No new technical arguments are introduced here; all

The purpose of this section is to make the logical structure of the reduction
explicit and to isolate the minimal algebraic primitives that must be simulated

3.1. Source Normal Form: ETR in Arithmetic-Circuit Equality Form
As established in Section 2.1, we work with the existential theory of the
reals(ETR)inthestandardbitmodel. ByLemma2.2, everyETRinstancecan
be transformed in polynomial time into an equisatisfiable instance consisting
solely of polynomial equalities.
Rather than working with explicit polynomial equations, we adopt the
arithmetic-circuit normal form formalized in Section 2.2. This representation
is standard in∃R-reductions and allows gate-by-gate simulation using local
algebraic constraints; see, e.g., [9, 15, 16].
Concretely, every instance is reduced to an arithmetic circuitC over R
with gate setGand designated output gateg out, such that

Each gate equation belongs to one of the following primitive types:

3.2. Target Problem: Fixed-Rank Affine Rank Minimization
The target decision problem is Affine Rank Minimization with fixed rank
bound k = 3, defined in Section 2.3. An instance consists of rational affine
constraints
⟨Aℓ,X⟩=b ℓ, ℓ∈[q],(3.3)
together with the global constraint
rank(X)≤3.(3.4)
The reduction constructs a single global matrix variableX∈R m×n whose
rank–3structure serves as a uniform carrier for all circuit scalars. Each circuit
12
variable is represented by a designated entry ofX, while all semantic relations
between variables are enforced using affine constraints combined with the
rank bound.
The subtlety is that hardness persists even when the rank is fixed to a
small constant. Fixed-rank algebraic feasibility problems are known to exhibit
∃R-hardness in a variety of geometric and matrix-realization settings; see, e.g.,
universality results stemming from Mnëv’s theorem and its refinements [18,
19].
3.3. Rank–3 as a Nonlinear Forcing Medium
A central principle of the reduction is that global rank constraints can
enforce nonlinear algebraic relations when combined with carefully placed
affineequalities. Thekeyobservationiselementarybutpowerful: if rank(X)≤
3, then every4×4minor ofXmust vanish.
Consequently, any collection of affine constraints that forces the existence
of a4× 4submatrix with nonzero determinant necessarily violates the rank
bound. This mechanism allows multiplication constraints of the formz =xy
to be enforced indirectly: one constructs a constant-size affine pattern such
that any violationz̸=xyinduces a nonzero determinant in a4×4minor.
Determinant-based rank obstructions of this type underlie universality
phenomena in realization spaces and related rank-constrained models [18, 19].
In the present work, this idea is distilled into a concrete rank–3forcing gadget
developed and analyzed rigorously in Section 5.

10. Conclusion
We established an exact real-algebraic complexity classification forAffine
Rank Minimizationunder a fixed constant rank bound. For every fixedk≥ 1,
ARM(k)∈∃R via an explicit existential encoding ofrank(X)≤k . Our main
42
result shows that this upper bound is tight already at rank3: we gave a
polynomial-time many-one reduction from ETR to ARM(3)in which the
output instance contains only rational affine equalities on the entries of asingle
matrix variableX, together with the single global constraintrank(X)≤3.
The reduction confines all nonlinearity to the rank bound. Linear circuit
semantics (constants, copying/fan-out, addition, negation) are enforced di-
rectly by affine constraints, while multiplication is enforced by a constant-size
determinant-forcing primitive: combined with a pinned full-rank3× 3gauge
block, a2× 2gadget yields a4 × 4minor whose determinant is a nonzero
constant multiple of(c−ab ), so any violation ofc =ab forces rank(X)≥ 4.
The pinned gauge also fixes theGL(3)ambiguity and enables a global com-
position argument, ensuring that all constant-size gadgets embed into one
global factorization X = UV without unintended coupling. This proves
soundness and completeness of the encoding and preserves polynomial bounds
on instance size and bit complexity.
Consequently, ARM(3)is ∃R-complete. In particular, deciding feasibility
of purely affine constraints underrank(X)≤ 3is as hard as real algebraic
feasibility, and thus (barring standard complexity collapses) admits no general
polynomial-time algorithm despite the fact that the only nonlinearity is the
rank constraint.
