## 1. Where the project stands (one paragraph)

The *characterization* of $q_{\min}$ is settled by the literature
([P1] arXiv:2609.33615, [P2] arXiv:2609.37992, Fernando 2026): existence, order spectrum
$\Sigma_{FO}$, per-order necessary-and-sufficient conditions ($\exists Z$ in P2 Thm 5, the chart
$Z_I(\Theta)$ in P2 Thm 6), $\nu_{\min}=\min\Sigma_{FO}$, and a construction. The only remaining
open problem — our $q1$ — is the **complexity** of the per-order feasibility test
$\mathrm{DEC}(q)$: decide whether there exists $\mathcal V\supseteq\operatorname{row}L_0$,
$\dim\mathcal V=q$, satisfying $(5a)$ and $(5b)$; equivalently whether there exists a chart $I$ and
$\Theta$ satisfying (14a)(14b).

Our own contribution is a *toolkit* around this question: lower bounds (A), equivalent forms of
$(5b)$ (B), exact values on special families (C, K), a computable lower-bound side $P=\rho_{ci}$
(D, E), a rank barrier (F), an $\exists\mathbb R$ upper bound for $\mathrm{DEC}(q)$ (G), and the
matrix/rank-to-quotient-pencil translation (H, I, J). **None of these settles $q1$.**

## 3. Questions about direction and correctness

**Q3.1 (direction).** We interpret the literature as: the only remaining problem is the complexity
of the chart/semialgebraic feasibility test $\mathrm{DEC}\text{-}\Theta$, and everything else in
our project is a (useful but non-decisive) toolkit. Is this reading correct? In particular, is it
correct that the per-order test in P2 Thm 6 **cannot** currently be evaluated in polynomial time,
and that no complexity class has been stated for it?

**Q3.2 (target class).** Since $\mathrm{NP}\subseteq\exists\mathbb R$ and we have
$\mathrm{DEC}(q)\in\exists\mathbb R$ (report G.1, chart-free encoding with $O(n^3)$ variables,
degree $\le n$), we believe the natural hardness target is **$\exists\mathbb R$-completeness**
(which would imply NP-hardness), rather than NP-hardness directly. Is this the right target, and is
there a known $\exists\mathbb R$-complete problem that is a natural reduction source for observer
design?

**Q3.3 (correctness of specific paper proofs).** Please check the paper arguments listed in
`workspace/questions_for_expert_2026-11-01.md`:

* **B.2** (top layer, $\rho=d$): the block identity and the passage
  "$\operatorname{rank}Q_{\mathcal V}(\lambda)=d\ \forall\lambda\iff$ no eigenvector in $\ker D_W$"
  via PBH. Are the degenerate cases $d=0$ or $c=0$ covered?
* **K.1** ($m=3$: every nonzero bivector decomposable): we argue via $w\wedge w=0$
  (which is automatic since $\Lambda^4=0$ in dimension 3) and the standard criterion
  "$w\wedge w=0\iff w$ decomposable". Is that acceptable, or do you prefer the
  $\mathrm{Gr}(2,3)=\mathbb P^2=\mathbb P(\Lambda^2\mathbb C^3)$ argument?
* **K.3** ($\ell\ge N-(2m-4)$): dimension counting of $\mathbb P(K)$ versus $\mathrm{Gr}(2,m)$.
  Is the projective intersection argument acceptable without further hypotheses?
* **D.1**: our statement uses the **per-$W$ bound** plus an infimum; the statement as written
  requires the feasible set to be nonempty (otherwise the infimum over $\mathbb N$ is $0$). Is the
  paper statement intended with $P=+\infty$ in the empty case?
* **J** (determinantal-divisor criterion): we would like to state it as
  "all $(r+t)$-minors have constant gcd $\iff$ rank is constant". Is the polynomial-ring version
  in $\mathbb C[\lambda]$ standard enough to cite, or does it need a proof?

**Q3.4 (novelty).** Which of our results do you consider possibly new (to be checked against the
literature before claiming)? Our candidates: the rank barrier
$\rho\le\min_\lambda r(\lambda)$ (F.1), the chart-free $\exists\mathbb R$ encoding of
$\mathrm{DEC}(q)$ (G.1), and the quotient-pencil equivalence (H.1).

## 4. Questions about the Lean formalization

**Q4.1 (modeling choices).** We work in the *subspace/dimension* language rather than the
*matrix/rank* language. We have Lean proofs bridging the two (`AlphaBridge`: column space of
$[T\mid Z]$ is $\operatorname{range}T\sqcup Z$; row rank $=$ column rank; $\operatorname{rank}A=$
$\dim$ row span $=$ $\dim$ col span), but the *matrix* itself is never a first-class object in our
statements. Is that acceptable for "verified" claims, or do you require the literal matrix-rank
formulation?

**Q4.2 (three specific "translations" we made).** Are these faithful?

1. **"$v$ is cyclic"** is formalized as $\operatorname{span}\{T^kv\}=W$, not as
   $\operatorname{rank}[v;vT;\dots;vT^{d-1}]=d$. (The rank form follows from the $\alpha$ bridge.)
2. **"$A^{\mathsf T}$ has an eigenvector $\varphi$"** is formalized as
   $\varphi\circ(\lambda I-A)=0$ on the dual, because Mathlib has no
   `dualMap_sub`/`dualMap_smul`; the two are the same object (`dualMap_apply` is `rfl`).
3. **"$P$"** is formalized as the infimum of $\dim W$ over feasible $W$, over $\mathbb N$, with an
   explicit nonemptiness hypothesis. The empty case ($P=+\infty$ in the paper) is not modeled.

**Q4.3 (how far should we go?).** Formalizing **J, K.1, K.3** requires infrastructure that Mathlib
does not have: minors and the "rank $=k\iff$ some $k$-minor $\ne 0$" bridge (J),
decomposability in $\Lambda^2$ and the $w\wedge w=0$ criterion (K.1), and
$\dim\mathrm{Gr}(2,m)=2(m-2)$ plus a projective-intersection dimension theorem (K.3). Each is a
self-contained project of its own. **Given that all three are already proven on paper, do you
recommend (a) continuing the formalization, or (b) spending the effort on the open problem
instead?** We are inclined to (b), but would like your view on what level of machine verification
is expected for a result like this.

**Q4.4 (B.2's matrix packaging).** B.2 is the one "structural" claim we could not finish: we have
the block form pointwise and PBH, but not the assembled matrix and the rank statement. It is a pure
modeling obstacle (choosing bases, aligning $\mathbb C^d$ with $W$), not a mathematical one. Do you
see any mathematical subtlety in that packaging that we might be missing?

## 5. Questions about the open problem

**Q5.1 (entry point).** Our notes propose starting from the layer $\rho=\rho_{ci}$, where
$\rho_{ci}=P(T,B,U_0)$ is computable in $O(n^4)$. The hope: if feasibility on that layer is
equivalent to "some polynomial does not vanish identically on an affine space", the layer test is
linear, hence $q1$ is false. Is this the best entry point, or would you start elsewhere?

**Q5.2 (general layer $0<\rho<d$).** For this layer we only have the pencil form and a
submatrix-gcd criterion; we also have positive results for $\rho=0$, $\rho=d$, $d=0$, $q=t$ and the
closure family. **What is missing, in your view, to decide this layer in polynomial time?** Is there
a structural reason (invariance, duality, matroid) that would make the general layer reducible to
the solved ones?

**Q5.3 (reduction).** For the hardness side: do you know of a reduction from a known hard problem
to the *observer* conditions $(5a)$–$(5b)$? The closest precedent we know is Löfberg's NP-hardness
of static output feedback (2026). Would adapting that reduction be the right first attempt, given
that our conditions are rank equalities in $\lambda$ (i.e., algebraic, closed) rather than
semialgebraic?

**Q5.4 (fixed order vs optimization).** We treat "decide whether a $q$-order solution exists" and
"decide $q_{\min}\le k$" as two separate problems, each needing its own reduction. Is that the
standard convention, and is there a logspace/parsimonious reduction between them that we should be
aware of?

**Q5.5 ($c_0$ interface).** In A.2/A.3 the constant $c_0$ is "the minimal dimension of an $S$
satisfying the $A$-side conditions". We did not formalize $c_0$ (it enters only through the
literature's $q_0=\sum_j\eta_j$). Is our reading of $c_0$ correct, and is $c_0$ really the same
object as the literature's $q_0$-related quantity?

**Q5.6 (E.1).** We claim $\rho_{ci}=P(T,B,U_0)$. Is this already in the literature (in which case we
should cite rather than prove), or is it worth publishing as a lemma?
