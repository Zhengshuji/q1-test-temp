### 4.1 Proof of the rank identity

Let

\\[ \pi:\mathbb C^n\longrightarrow \mathbb C^n/\operatorname{row}L_0. \\]

Then

\\[ \rank[L_0;R] = \rank L_0+\rank(\pi R). \\]

Therefore

\\[ \rank[L_0;R]=r+m=q \\]

is equivalent to

\\[ \rank L_0=r,\qquad \rank(\pi R)=m. \\]

If \\(L_0\\) does not have full row rank, no \\(R\\) can produce a stacked matrix of rank \\(q\\). This should be stated as an initial infeasibility test.

There is also the necessary dimension condition

\\[ m\leq n-r, \\]

equivalently \\(q\leq n\\).

### 4.2 Symbolic-rank equivalence

Suppose \\(\mathcal R\ne\varnothing\\). Write

\\[ R(t)=R_0+\sum\_{j=1}^{s}t_jR_j, \\]

where \\(R_1,\ldots,R_s\\) span the homogeneous solution space.

The entries of \\(\pi R(t)\\) are affine polynomials in \\(t_1,\ldots,t_s\\).

Then the following are equivalent:

1. There exists \\(t\in\mathbb C^s\\) with \\(\rank\pi R(t)=m\\).
2. At least one \\(m\times m\\) minor is a nonzero polynomial.
3. \\(\rank\pi R(t)=m\\) over \\(\mathbb C(t_1,\ldots,t_s)\\).

The first equivalence uses the fact that a nonzero polynomial over an infinite field cannot vanish everywhere.

Thus the symbolic-rank test is exact.

### 4.3 The open-dense conclusion

If one maximal minor is a nonzero polynomial, the set

\\[ \\{t:\rank\pi R(t)=m\\} \\]

is the union of the nonvanishing loci of the maximal minors. It is a nonempty Zariski-open subset of the affine parameter space.

Since affine space is irreducible, this set is dense.

Hence:

\\[ \boxed{ \mathcal R\_{\rm full} \text{ is either empty or Zariski-open dense in }\mathcal R. } \tag{17} \\]

This is the strongest general algebraic conclusion.

### 4.4 Generalise the lemma

I recommend stating the result for an arbitrary affine matrix space.

Affine full-row-rank lemma. Let

\\[ \mathcal A=R_0+\mathcal V\subseteq\mathbb C^{m\times n} \\]

be a nonempty affine subspace, and let \\(\pi:\mathbb C^n\to\mathbb C^N\\) be a fixed linear map. Then

\\[ \mathcal A\_{\rm full} = \\{R\in\mathcal A:\rank(\pi R)=m\\} \\]

is either empty or a nonempty Zariski-open dense subset of \\(\mathcal A\\). It is nonempty if and only if the generic matrix \\(\pi R(t)\\) has row rank \\(m\\) over the rational function field of the affine parameter space.

The observer lifting lemma follows immediately by taking \\(\pi\\) to be the quotient by \\(\operatorname{row}L_0\\).

## 6. The restricted \\(d=2\\) all-bad theorem: a complete proof worth retaining

\\[ \rank Z_2=2. \\]

\\[ \ker(\cdot Z_2)=\operatorname{span}\\{\ell\\} \\]

\\[ NZ_1+ANZ_2=0. \\]

\\[ U_0Z_1+V_0Z_2=0. \\]

\\[ V_0Z_2=0. \\]

For an admissible point:

- \\(\omega=0\\): automatically good;
- \\(\omega=1\\): good exactly when \\(Z(W)\not\subseteq W\\);
- \\(\omega=2\\): always bad over \\(\mathbb C\\), since every complex square matrix has an eigenvector.

Therefore \\(q=2\\) is feasible if and only if there exists an admissible \\(\omega=0\\) point or a good admissible \\(\omega=1\\) point.

One must not infer the minimum feasible order by checking only the lowest-dimensional rank stratum. Every admissible \\(\omega\\)-stratum must be considered.

## 9. Which generalisation should come next?

I would slightly refine my previous recommendation.

### Priority 1: \\(k=3\\), \\(e>0\\)

This is the most valuable immediate generalisation. It tests whether the projective theorem survives the presence of fixed top rows and forces the use of the full condition

\\[ \widetilde E_S+X_S=\mathbb C^\omega. \\]

It is also the cheapest way to test whether the observed component geometry is genuinely structural.

### Priority 2: \\(k>3\\), small \\(\omega\\)

The natural parameter space becomes

\\[ \operatorname{Gr}(\omega,k). \\]

The compatibility and admissibility conditions can be formulated as degeneracy loci of bundle maps over this Grassmannian.

This is the natural route to a general theorem.

### Priority 3: real-point feasibility

This is essential for applications over \\(\mathbb R\\), but it should be treated as a separate theorem.

For example, if a complex irreducible component is defined over \\(\mathbb R\\), has a nonsingular real point, and is geometrically irreducible, then its real points are Zariski dense in that component. In such a situation, a nonempty Zariski-open good locus intersects the real points.

But without a suitable real-point hypothesis, complex generic feasibility does not imply real feasibility.

### Priority 4: \\(q>3\\)

This is important but should follow the preceding work. Increasing \\(q\\) changes the dimension of the complement \\(W'\\), and the badness criterion becomes

\\[ \exists\\,0\ne y\in\ker B,\quad Ay\in\mathbb Cy, \\]

with \\(B\\) now having \\(q-\omega\\) rows.

The \\(q=3,\omega=2\\) case is special because \\(\ker B\\) is generically one-dimensional, making the criterion particularly transparent.
