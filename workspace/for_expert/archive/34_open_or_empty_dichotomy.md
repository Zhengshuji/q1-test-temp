# $q1$ progress: the feasible locus is open-or-empty, and what that makes the problem

This report continues the $q1$ programme only. It records four things obtained since the previous
report, including one retraction and two repairs, and asks three questions.

---

# Part I. Setting (restated)

## I.1 Problem

Given $A\in\mathbb C^{n\times n}$, $C\in\mathbb C^{p\times n}$, $L_0\in\mathbb C^{r\times n}$, and $q\ge r$,
let $\mathcal L=\begin{bmatrix}L_0\\R\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb C^{q\times n}$ and require

$$(5a)\ \operatorname{rank}\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal LA\\ \mathcal L\\ CA\\ C\end{bmatrix}
=\operatorname{rank}\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal L\\ CA\\ C\end{bmatrix},
\qquad
(5b)\ \operatorname{rank}\bigl[\lambda\mathcal L-\mathcal LA;\ CA;\ C\bigr]=h\ \ \forall\lambda\in\mathbb C,$$

$h=\operatorname{rank}[\mathcal L;CA;C]$. $\mathrm{DEC}(q)$: does there exist $R\in\mathbb R^{(q-r)\times n}$
satisfying $(5a)$ and $(5b)$? Question $q1$: is computing $q_{\min}$ NP-hard?

## I.2 Notation

$K=\ker[C;CA]$, $k=\dim K$; $N_K$ a rational basis of $K$ (as columns); $H(R)=\mathcal L(R)N_K$,
$H_A(R)=\mathcal LA(R)N_K$, both $q\times k$ and both linear in $R$; $\omega=\operatorname{rank}H(R)$;
$W_K=\mathcal L(R)K=\operatorname{im}H(R)$.

# Part II. Results

## II.1 The criterion, in its correct rectangular form

$$(5b)\iff\gcd\bigl\{\text{all }\omega\times\omega\text{ minors of }\lambda H(R)-H_A(R)\bigr\}\in\mathbb C^\times .$$

The minors are those of a $q\times\omega$ pencil, so there are $\binom{q}{\omega}$ of them.
Verified on explicit data: for
$A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\-1&-1&-1\\-1&-1&-1\end{pmatrix}$, $C=e_1^{\mathsf T}$, $L_0=e_3^{\mathsf T}$, $q=2$,
$K=\langle e_3\rangle$, $\omega=1$: with $R=(-1,-1,-1)$ the two minors are $\lambda+1$ and $-\lambda-2$
(gcd $=1$, so $(5b)$ holds), while with $R=(0,0,0)$ they are $\lambda+1$ and $0$ (gcd $=\lambda+1$, so $(5b)$ fails).

## II.2 A retraction

We previously claimed: if $\omega=\min(q,k)$ then $(5b)$ fails. The case $q\le k$ is the square case and is
correct (a square pencil's determinant has a root). The case $k<q$ is **wrong**: for a rectangular pencil, one
vanishing $\omega$-minor does not imply rank drop elsewhere. A finite search produced 8748 counterexamples; for
instance $A=\begin{pmatrix}0&1&0\\-1&-1&-1\\-1&-1&-1\end{pmatrix}$, $C=e_1^{\mathsf T}$, $L_0=e_3^{\mathsf T}$,
$q=2$, $R=(-1,-1,-1)$ is feasible, though $k=\omega=1$. The claim is withdrawn; only the square case
($\omega=q>0\Rightarrow(5b)$ fails) is retained.

## II.3 Repairs to the family-level formulation

Two errors of the same origin (confusing square with rectangular) are repaired:

1. charts must be chosen by **columns** of $H$ (since $W_K=\operatorname{im}H$), not by rows;
2. within a chart the criterion is the gcd of the $\binom{q}{\omega}$ minors, not a single determinant.

The family-level description is therefore: stratify by $\omega$; on each stratum cover by
$\binom{k}{\omega}$ column charts; in a chart the minors, after clearing the denominator $\det H_{[:,S]}$,
become polynomials in $R$, and the condition "gcd is a nonzero constant" becomes solvability of a Bézout
linear system, i.e. a rank condition — hence a constructible set.

## II.4 The dichotomy that organises everything (main new point)

The condition "all $\omega$-minors have a common complex root" is **closed**: it is the zero set of the
elimination ideal $I\cap\mathbb C[R]$, where $I$ is generated by the minors in $\mathbb C[R,\lambda]$.
Hence on each stratum-chart

$$\mathcal B:=\{R:\text{ common root exists}\}\ \text{is closed},\qquad \text{feasible set}=U\setminus\mathcal B\ \text{is open}.$$

So on an irreducible stratum-chart exactly one of two things happens:
$$\mathcal B=U\ \ (\text{every candidate in the chart is infeasible}),\qquad
\mathcal B\subsetneq U\ \ (\text{feasible set is Zariski-open dense}).$$

There is no intermediate case. This is consistent with our explicit computations: the feasible instance had
feasible locus $=\{r_3=0\}\setminus\{r_4=0\}$, open dense in the necessary hyperplane; and a batch of twelve
instances was infeasible in every chart.

**Consequences for $q1$.** Deciding feasibility of a chart is deciding whether the elimination ideal is proper
(and unsaturated) on that chart — an ideal-theoretic question, not a search problem. It also explains an
asymmetry we observed empirically: a single random feasible $R$ certifies feasibility (because the locus is
open), while failing to find one proves nothing.

## II.5 What the problem is and is not

Our family is a proper subfamily of affine pencil zero exclusion (the two coefficient matrices share the same
$\mathcal L(R)$, with fixed right multipliers $N_K$ and $AN_K$) and a proper subfamily of polynomial-matrix
left-prime completion (the pencil is of degree one and the parameters enter linearly). We do not know whether
either general problem reduces to ours. Accordingly,

$$q_{\min}=\min\{q:\ \exists\,\omega<\min(q,k)\ \exists S:\ \mathcal B_S^{(q)}\subsetneq U_S^{(q)}\},$$

i.e. the least order at which some stratum-chart has a proper elimination ideal. The remaining difficulty is
therefore (i) deciding properness of these elimination ideals and (ii) the combinatorial choice of strata and
charts.

# Part III. Questions

**Question 1.** Is the dichotomy in II.4 correct as stated? In particular: is "the minors have a common
complex root" really the vanishing of $I\cap\mathbb C[R]$ (so that the bad set is closed), or does the
elimination only give the Zariski closure of the bad set? If the latter, the dichotomy becomes
"empty, or open dense, or a proper closed set" — we would like to know which is true.

**Question 2.** If the dichotomy holds, then $q_{\min}$ is the least order at which an elimination ideal is
proper. Is this a known type of question with known complexity? Our own search did not locate a result on the
complexity of "deciding whether the elimination ideal of a parametric pencil is proper".

**Question 3.** Given the dichotomy, does it make sense to attack $q1$ by proving that for some $q$ the
elimination ideal is proper *for all* plant data (an algorithm), or that it is unit *for all* plant data of a
certain shape (a hardness construction)? In other words: is the right object the chart, the stratum, or the
whole decision problem?

We remain uninterested in writing up until $q1$ moves; the retraction and the repairs above are reported
because they were errors, not because they are results.
