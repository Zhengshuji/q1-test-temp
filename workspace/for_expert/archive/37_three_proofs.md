# Three standalone proofs for review: the reduced criterion, the closedness of the bad locus, and the corrected Bézout system

This report contains no new programme, only three self-contained proofs written in the form requested in the
last audit, and three questions about them.

---

# Part I. Setting

$H,H_A\in\mathbb C^{q\times k}$ arise as $H=\mathcal L(R)N_K$, $H_A=\mathcal LA(R)N_K$ with
$K=\ker[C;CA]$, but the three statements below are stated for arbitrary $H,H_A$, so no observer data are
needed. Write $\omega=\operatorname{rank}H$ and $P(R,\lambda)=\lambda H(R)-H_A(R)$.

Throughout, $(5a)$ is used in the equivalent form $\ker H\subseteq\ker H_A$ (machine-checked form:
$K\cap\ker L\le\ker T\iff\ker(L|_K)\le\ker(T|_K)$).

# Part II. Three proofs

## II.1 The reduced criterion

**Theorem 1.** Assume $\ker H\subseteq\ker H_A$ and let $\omega=\operatorname{rank}H$.

* If $\omega=0$, then $H=0$ and $H_A=0$, and $(5b)$ holds (the reduced pencil is empty).
* If $\omega>0$, then the normal rank of $P$ is $\omega$ and
$$(5b)\iff\gcd\{\text{all }\omega\times\omega\text{ minors of }P\}\in\mathbb C^\times
\iff\operatorname{rank}P(\lambda_0)=\omega\ \text{for every }\lambda_0\in\mathbb C .$$

*Proof.* Since $H_A$ vanishes on $\ker H$, it factors through $\operatorname{im}H$: there is
$Z\in\mathbb C^{q\times q}$ with
$$H_A=ZH .$$
Then $P(\lambda)=(\lambda I-Z)H$. For any $\lambda_0$,
$$\operatorname{rank}P(\lambda_0)\le\operatorname{rank}H=\omega,\qquad
\ker P(\lambda_0)=\{x: Hx\in\ker(\lambda_0I-Z)\}\supseteq\ker H,$$
so $\operatorname{rank}P(\lambda_0)=\omega\iff\ker(\lambda_0I-Z)\cap\operatorname{im}H=\{0\}$.
Choosing $\lambda_0$ outside the spectrum of $Z$ makes $\lambda_0I-Z$ invertible, hence
$\operatorname{rank}P(\lambda_0)=\omega$; this proves that the normal rank is $\omega$. Finally, for a fixed
$\lambda_0$, $\operatorname{rank}P(\lambda_0)\ge\omega$ iff some $\omega$-minor is nonzero; the minors have no
common root over $\mathbb C$ iff their gcd is a constant. $\square$

*Remark.* The obstruction is now transparent: $(5b)$ fails iff $Z$ has an eigenvector inside
$\operatorname{im}H=W_K$, and $\Phi_K=Z|_{\operatorname{im}H}$. This agrees with the invariant-line criterion
obtained earlier by a different route.

## II.2 Closedness of the bad locus

**Theorem 2.** Let $U$ be an algebraic set on which $\operatorname{rank}H(R)=\omega\ge1$ is constant. Then
$$\mathcal B=\{R\in U:\ \exists\lambda_0\in\mathbb C,\ \operatorname{rank}P(R,\lambda_0)<\omega\}$$
is Zariski closed in $U$.

*Proof.* Put $P^{\mathrm h}(R;\alpha,\beta)=\alpha H(R)-\beta H_A(R)$ and
$$\mathcal X=\bigl\{(R,[\alpha:\beta])\in U\times\mathbb P^1:\ \text{all }\omega\text{-minors of }
P^{\mathrm h}(R;\alpha,\beta)\text{ vanish}\bigr\}.$$
(i) $\mathcal X$ is closed, being defined by polynomials in $R$ times homogeneous forms in $(\alpha,\beta)$.
(ii) At $[1:0]$ we have $P^{\mathrm h}=H(R)$, and $\operatorname{rank}H(R)=\omega$ makes some $\omega$-minor
nonzero, so $\mathcal X$ meets no point at infinity; in particular $\beta\ne0$ on $\mathcal X$.
(iii) The projection $\mathrm{pr}_1:U\times\mathbb P^1\to U$ is proper because $\mathbb P^1$ is complete, hence
closed; so $\mathrm{pr}_1(\mathcal X)$ is closed.
(iv) By (ii), $R\in\mathrm{pr}_1(\mathcal X)$ iff there is $\lambda_0=\alpha/\beta$ with all $\omega$-minors of
$P(R,\lambda_0)$ vanishing, i.e. $\operatorname{rank}P(R,\lambda_0)<\omega$. Hence
$\mathcal B=\mathrm{pr}_1(\mathcal X)$. $\square$

**Corollary 1.** If $U$ is irreducible, then $\mathcal B=U$ or $\mathcal B\subsetneq U$; in the second case
$U\setminus\mathcal B$ is a nonempty Zariski-open dense subset.

**Corollary 2.** Let $A$ be the coordinate ring of $U$ (a domain), $J$ the ideal generated by the
$\omega$-minors in $A[\lambda]$, and $E=J\cap A$. Then $\mathcal B=V(E)$ and
$$E=(0)\iff\mathcal B=U,\qquad 0\ne E\ne A\iff\mathcal B\text{ proper closed},\qquad E=A\iff\mathcal B=\varnothing .$$
The feasibility test is $E\ne(0)$, not "$E$ proper".

*Remark on the hypothesis.* Full column rank of the leading coefficient cannot be dropped: for
$I=\langle R\lambda-1\rangle$ the bad set is $\mathbb C\setminus\{0\}$, which is not closed, and the
elimination ideal is zero, so elimination alone gives only the closure.

## II.3 The corrected Bézout system

**Theorem 3.** Fix $\omega>0$ on a chart and let $p_1,\dots,p_N$ ($N=\binom q\omega$) be the
$\omega$-minors of $P$, each of degree at most $\omega$ in $\lambda$ with coefficients of degree at most
$\omega$ in $R$. Then
$$\gcd(p_1,\dots,p_N)=1\iff\exists u_1,\dots,u_N,\ \deg u_i\le\omega-1,\ \sum_i u_ip_i=1
\iff M_\omega(R)\,u=b_\omega\ \text{is solvable},$$
where $u$ collects the coefficients of the $u_i$ (there are $N\omega$ unknowns),
$$M_\omega(R)\in\mathbb C^{2\omega\times N\omega},\qquad b_\omega=(1,0,\dots,0)^{\mathsf T},$$
and the $2\omega$ rows correspond to the coefficients of $\lambda^0,\dots,\lambda^{2\omega-1}$

*Proof.* The bound on the degrees of $u_i$ is the bounded-degree Bézout lemma.
For $\deg u_i\le\omega-1$ and $\deg p_i\le\omega$ one has $\deg\bigl(\sum_i u_ip_i\bigr)\le2\omega-1$, so
the identity is equivalent to matching $2\omega$ coefficients; each matching equation is linear in the unknown
coefficients of the $u_i$ with coefficients taken from the $p_i$, i.e. from the data. $\square$

**Corollary (inconsistency is a rank comparison, not a minor condition).** On the chart,
$$\mathcal B_\omega=\{R:\operatorname{rank}[M_\omega(R)\mid b_\omega]>\operatorname{rank}M_\omega(R)\},$$
and this is an equality of sets, not an equality up to closure. A union of rank strata is needed to write it:
$$\mathcal B_\omega=\bigcup_{s=0}^{2\omega-1}\{R:\operatorname{rank}M_\omega(R)=s,\ \operatorname{rank}[M_\omega(R)\mid b_\omega]\ge s+1\}.$$
The set $\{R:\text{all maximal minors of }[M\mid b]\text{ vanish}\}$ is **not** the bad set: if
$\operatorname{rank}M=m-1$ and $\operatorname{rank}[M\mid b]=m$ the system is inconsistent yet the augmented
matrix has a nonzero maximal minor.

**Degree bounds.** The entries of $M_\omega$ have degree at most $\omega$ in $R$; a minor of size $s$ has degree
at most $s\omega$, and $M_\omega$ has $2\omega$ rows, so the rank-stratified equations have degree at most
$$2\omega^2\quad\text{in }R .$$
In the factor parameterisation $H=UV^{\mathsf T}$, $H_A=ZV^{\mathsf T}$ (which is exactly the condition
$\ker H\subseteq\ker H_A$), the pencil is $(\lambda U-Z)V^{\mathsf T}$, each maximal minor factors as
$\det((\lambda U-Z)_{I,:})\det(V_{J,:}^{\mathsf T})$, and the pullback of a rank condition has total degree at
most
$$4\omega^2\quad\text{in }(U,V,Z).$$

# Part III. Questions

**Question 1.** Are Theorems 1–3 correct as stated? Theorem 1 rests on the factorisation $H_A=ZH$, which we
believe follows from $\ker H\subseteq\ker H_A$; Theorem 2 on properness of the projection from
$U\times\mathbb P^1$; Theorem 3 on the coefficient count $2\omega$. We would like each of these three points
checked as an identity, not as a sketch.

**Question 2.** Is the factorisation $H_A=ZH$ with a **square** $Z\in\mathbb C^{q\times q}$ the right form for
the observer problem, or should $Z$ be allowed to be rectangular (i.e. a map $\operatorname{im}H\to
\operatorname{im}H_A$)? If rectangular suffices, Theorem 1's proof simplifies, but we would like to know
whether the square form is used anywhere in the audit.

**Question 3.** Of the three, which is most likely to hide an error? We ask so that we can spend the next round
on that one alone. Our own guess is Theorem 3's corollary, because the passage from "linear system" to
"rank-stratified constructible description" is where our earlier attempt failed.
