Academic Writing Review Report
Manuscript: Parameterized Algorithms for Functional Observer Feasibility

Document type: Research paper in control theory, computational mathematics, and parameterized algorithm design.

Overall assessment: 
Partially meets academic writing requirements

The manuscript has a recognizable academic-paper structure, a clearly identified research problem, substantial mathematical content, and a generally appropriate scholarly writing style. Its organization follows a conventional research-paper format, and it makes a serious attempt to distinguish existing functional-observer characterizations from the new computational-complexity contributions.

However, the manuscript is not yet ready for submission in its current form. The most important concerns are not basic language or formatting defects, but the consistency of the mathematical claims, the completeness of the algorithmic complexity arguments, the reliability of the worked example, and the precision with which the contributions are presented.

In particular, the worked example contains an explicit inconsistency concerning the minimum observer order. This needs to be resolved before the paper can be considered academically rigorous.

The assessment below distinguishes between issues directly identifiable in the manuscript and matters that require further mathematical verification.

2. Structural logic and organization
2.1 Strengths
The overall organization is appropriate for a theoretical computer science and control-theory paper:
1. Introduction
2. Preliminaries
3. Compressed pencil
4. Effective free-row dimension
5. Rank factorization and finite certificate
6. Fixed-order algorithm
7. Fixed-parameter algorithm
8. Membership in the existential theory of the reals
9. Conclusions
10. Appendices
This sequence has a reasonable mathematical progression:
- Establish the original observer-feasibility conditions.
- Compress the problem using the common kernel.
- Identify the effective dimension.
- Construct a finite algebraic certificate.
- Derive computational complexity bounds.
- Discuss the existential-real formulation and limitations.
The distinction between the fixed-order and fixed-parameter algorithms is particularly valuable. They establish different types of complexity guarantees and should remain separate.

The manuscript also makes a good effort to distinguish the existing feasibility characterization from the new algorithmic contributions. This is important for establishing originality.
2.2 Main structural problems
Problem 1: The abstract and introduction do not accurately reflect the algorithms' treatment of Grassmann charts.

The abstract states:
no enumeration of charts or of minors is required.

The introduction similarly suggests that the proposed approach avoids enumeration of chart parameters.

However, Algorithm 1 in the fixed-parameter section explicitly contains:
\For{each chart of $\mathrm{Gr}(t,m')$ with chart coordinates ...}


The proof of Theorem thm:fpt subsequently establishes the chart-count bound
\[
\sum_t \binom{m'}{t}=2^{m'}\leq 4^\ell.
\]

Thus, the manuscript does enumerate Grassmann charts in its fixed-parameter algorithm, although the enumeration is bounded by a function of the parameter \(\ell\).

This is not necessarily a mathematical error in the algorithm. It is a discrepancy in how the contribution is described.

Suggested revision:

Distinguish the two claims explicitly:
- The finite spectral certificate avoids enumerating maximal minors.
- The fixed-order algorithm avoids enumerating Grassmann charts.
- The fixed-parameter algorithm permits chart enumeration, but bounds its number by \(4^\ell\).
This distinction would make the paper's main contribution more accurate.

Problem 2: The relationship between the main results could be presented more clearly.

The paper contains several distinct results:
- Compression to a reduced problem.
- Identification of \(\ell\).
- A spectral lower bound.
- A finite certificate.
- Fixed-order complexity.
- Fixed-parameter tractability.
- Minimum-order computation.
- Observer construction.
- Existential-real membership.
All are relevant, but the introduction gives them considerable weight before the reader has fully understood the mathematical setting.

I recommend introducing a compact overview of the logical dependencies.

For example:
Recommended logical structure
Original observer feasibility conditions


Common-kernel compression


Effective subspace representation and parameter \(\ell\)


Finite spectral certificate

Cauchy–Binet compression


Subspace reduction

Bounded Grassmann dimension




Fixed-order algorithm

Polynomial degree depending on \(q\)


Fixed-parameter algorithm

Parameter-dependent factor in \(\ell\)




Optimization, construction, and complexity classification


This would help readers understand which results depend on the certificate and which depend on the subspace reduction.

Problem 3: The worked example should be better integrated into the paper.

The example is currently placed after the existential-real encoding appendix. This is not ideal for a central example that illustrates the main algorithm and challenges an existing published or preprint result.

I recommend moving the worked example to the main text, immediately after the fixed-parameter algorithm, or presenting a shorter version there and retaining the complete calculations in the appendix.

3.2 Language issues requiring attention
A. Some statements are unnecessarily emphatic.

For example:
The error is not in the characterization but in the way it is evaluated there.

And:
That report is in error.

These statements concern the discrepancy with the work cited as fer26b. Although a mathematical correction may be entirely justified, the wording should remain neutral and evidence-based.

A more appropriate formulation would be:
Our explicit feasible construction appears to contradict the infeasibility reported in Section VII-B of \(\cite{fer26b}\). We provide the corresponding exact rank and polynomial-pencil certificates below. This discrepancy warrants a verification of the computational procedure used in that reference.

This wording is preferable because it:
1. States the mathematical discrepancy.
2. Identifies the supporting evidence.
3. Avoids attributing the discrepancy to a particular methodological error without sufficient evidence.
4. Maintains an objective scholarly tone.
B. Some claims are repeated too frequently.

The following ideas appear repeatedly:
- \(\ell\) is independent of \(r\).
- The chart parameterization grows with the state dimension.
- The new method avoids enumerating maximal minors.
- The unrestricted complexity remains open.
- The construction algorithm lacks a bit-complexity guarantee.
These repetitions are understandable because they are central to the paper. However, the introduction, contributions, conclusions, and limitations sections could be more concise.

I recommend keeping:
- The full explanation in the introduction.
- The formal statements in the relevant theorem sections.
- A concise summary in the conclusions.
C. Some sentences are too long.

Several paragraphs in the introduction combine multiple mathematical claims, comparisons, and qualifications in a single sentence.

For instance, the discussion of the two recent functional-observer papers is quite dense.

Consider dividing long sentences into:
1. What the cited work establishes.
2. What computational question remains unresolved.
3. What the present paper contributes.
This would improve readability without reducing technical precision.

D. Terminology should be standardized.

The manuscript uses several related expressions:
- observer rows;
- free rows;
- free observer rows;
- fixed functionals;
- fixed rows;
- row functionals;
- compressed row pairs.
These are mathematically related but should have clearly defined meanings.

In particular, I recommend establishing a consistent distinction between:
- prescribed observer rows \(L_0\);
- free observer rows \(R\);
- the full observer matrix \(L=[L_0;R]\);
- the compressed matrices \(H=LN\) and \(H_A=LAN\);
- the subspace \(W=\operatorname{row}[H\ H_A]\).
After these definitions, use the same terminology throughout.

5. Rigor of argumentation and mathematical presentation
Requires further verification
This is the most important part of the review.

The manuscript contains substantial mathematical development. It does not simply state algorithms and complexity bounds; it attempts to establish them through a sequence of lemmas, propositions, and theorems.

Nevertheless, several issues need attention.
5.1 The main mathematical development
The paper's argument has a generally appropriate structure:
- Lemma-based compression of the original observer conditions.
- Characterization of attainable row-pair spaces.
- Reduction from free observer rows to a subspace \(W\).
- Identification of the effective parameter \(\ell\).
- Construction of a finite spectral certificate.
- Application of real-algebraic decision procedures.
The distinction between the following two quantities is particularly important:
\[
\kappa_{\mathrm{obs}}=r d_0,
\qquad
\ell=\operatorname{rank}(AN).
\]

The paper explains that the first describes the number of coordinates needed to represent the free rows, whereas the second controls the fixed-parameter complexity bound.

This is a meaningful conceptual contribution, and the manuscript appropriately emphasizes it.
5.2 The worked example contains a significant logical inconsistency
Major revision required
This is the clearest identifiable problem in the current manuscript.

In Appendix B, the paper first states:
\[
q_{\min}=4.
\]

It argues that:
- \(\mathrm{DEC}(4)\) is feasible.
- \(\mathrm{DEC}(3)\) is infeasible.
- The lower bound establishes that one free row cannot suffice.
However, the final paragraph subsequently states:
We do not claim \(q_{\min}=4\) for this instance: that would require \(\mathrm{DEC}(3)\) to return infeasible, which we have not checked.

These statements cannot both be maintained as written.

There are two possible resolutions.

Option A: The lower-bound argument is complete.

If the proposition cited in the example rigorously establishes that no feasible observer exists at order three, and the order-two case is also excluded, then the paper can retain
\[
q_{\min}=4.
\]

The final qualification should then be removed.

Option B: The lower-bound argument is insufficient or has not been verified.

In that case, the manuscript should withdraw the equality and state only
\[
q_{\min}\leq 4.
\]

It can still conclude that the order \(5\) reported in the cited work cannot be the minimum, because an explicit feasible order-four observer has been provided.

The authors must establish which situation is correct. This is not merely a stylistic issue: it concerns the logical validity of a stated result.
5.3 The claimed discrepancy with the existing literature needs stronger documentation
The manuscript states that the result in fer26b is incorrect for the worked example.

It provides an explicit rational observer matrix and reports:
\[
\operatorname{rank}[L;CA;C]
=
\operatorname{rank}[LA;L;CA;C]
=7.
\]

It also reports that all 64 maximal minors of the associated pencil are nonzero and have gcd one.

This is useful evidence.

However, because the manuscript challenges an existing result, the evidence should be presented in a particularly reproducible way.

I recommend including:
1. The complete numerical matrix \(A\), rather than relying exclusively on the phrase "as printed there."
2. The exact rational entries of the proposed observer matrix.
3. The computed compressed matrices \(H\) and \(H_A\).
4. A reproducible verification of the algebraic condition.
5. A reproducible verification of the spectral condition.
6. The exact calculation establishing the gcd of the maximal minors.
7. A clear explanation of whether the disagreement concerns feasibility at order four or the actual minimum order.
The last distinction is essential.

An order-four feasible observer establishes \(q_{\min}\leq4\), but does not alone establish \(q_{\min}=4\).
5.4 The complexity analysis contains acknowledged proof obligations
The paper is commendably transparent about its limitations.

In the conclusions, it explicitly acknowledges:
- The bit complexity of the construction algorithm is not established.
- The bit-complexity audit of the zero-free-row case is incomplete.
- The coefficient-size lemma has not been independently audited.
This transparency is a strength.

However, these qualifications also affect the strength of the paper's computational claims.

The manuscript should distinguish clearly between:
Result	Current status as presented
Fixed-order decision algorithm	Bit-complexity bound claimed
Fixed-parameter decision algorithm	Bit-complexity bound claimed
Minimum-order computation	Derived from decision algorithm
Observer construction	Arithmetic-model guarantee
Bit complexity of construction	Not established
Zero-free-row polynomial-time test	Exact algorithm claimed; detailed bit-bound audit remains
Coefficient-size estimates	Proof provided; constants not independently audited

In particular, the following distinction should be maintained throughout:
\[
\text{Arithmetic complexity}
\neq
\text{Bit complexity}.
\]

A construction algorithm that returns exact real algebraic numbers in an arithmetic model does not automatically establish a polynomial bit-complexity bound.

The abstract and conclusions generally recognize this distinction, but it should be checked throughout the paper to ensure that no stronger guarantee is inadvertently implied.
5.5 The fixed-parameter algorithm needs especially careful exposition
The proposed bound is
\[
T\leq
\exp\bigl(O(\ell^2\log(\ell+2))\bigr)
\operatorname{poly}(\mathcal L).
\]

This is an important result.

The proof relies on several components:
- The dimension restriction on feasible \(W\).
- The bound \(\omega\leq\ell\).
- The restriction \(t\leq2\ell\).
- The bounded Grassmann chart dimension.
- The real-algebraic decision procedure.
- The coefficient-size estimate.
The argument is logically organized, but the transition from the geometric reduction to the computational bound is sufficiently technical that readers need a more explicit explanation of the complexity accounting.

I recommend adding a summary table:
Quantity	Bound
Ambient subspace dimension \(m'\)	\(m'\leq2\ell-s_0\)
Grassmann chart dimension	\(t(m'-t)\leq\ell^2\)
Certificate variables	\(2\omega\leq2\ell\)
Number of charts	At most \(4^\ell\)
Polynomial degree	\(O(\ell)\)
Number of polynomial constraints	Polynomial in \(\ell\)
Coefficient bit length	Polynomial in \(\mathcal L\), subject to the coefficient-size lemma

This would make the origin of the final complexity bound much easier to verify.
5.6 Formal verification claims need precise boundaries
The section on Lean 4 and Mathlib is a positive feature.

The manuscript distinguishes:
- Literal formalizations.
- Abstract-core formalizations.
- Individual proof ingredients.
- Paper-level arguments.
This is a responsible approach to reporting formal verification.

However, the paper should avoid allowing readers to infer that the entire main theorem has been formally verified.

The accompanying table already helps, but I recommend adding a short statement such as:
The Lean development verifies the specified algebraic and linear-algebraic components, but does not constitute a formal verification of the complete algorithmic complexity theorems.

The repository should also identify a reproducible version or commit corresponding to the manuscript.
5.7 Existential-real membership
The paper's claim
\[
\mathrm{DEC}(q)\in\exists\mathbb R
\]

and its extension to the finite threshold problem are important.

The appendix provides a detailed encoding, including:
- Rank factorization.
- Polynomial left inverses.
- Degree bounds.
- Input-consistency constraints.
- Soundness and completeness arguments.
This is a strong aspect of the mathematical presentation.

Nevertheless, the soundness and completeness proof should receive an independent final audit, especially the correspondence between the encoded variables and the original free observer rows.

The manuscript correctly emphasizes that the input-consistency equations cannot be omitted. This is important because otherwise the encoding would describe arbitrary compressed matrix pairs rather than pairs realizable by the original observer.

I recommend retaining this explanation.

8. Recommended revision strategy
I recommend addressing the issues in the following order.

Stage 1: Mathematical consistency
1. Resolve the contradiction concerning the minimum order in Appendix B.
2. Recheck the explicit observer witness and its two feasibility conditions.
3. Verify the claimed discrepancy with the existing literature.
4. Reconcile the abstract and introduction with the actual chart-enumeration procedure.
Stage 2: Proof completeness
1. Audit the coefficient-size lemma.
2. Complete the bit-complexity analysis of the zero-free-row case.
3. Clearly delimit the arithmetic-model guarantees for construction.
4. Check the soundness and completeness of the existential-real encoding.
5. Verify that the complexity bounds follow under the stated rational-input model.

1. Improve the literature review.
2. Reduce repetition in the introduction and conclusions.
3. Improve the explanation of the fixed-parameter complexity analysis.

The manuscript demonstrates a substantial level of academic preparation and has the structure and mathematical presentation expected of a theoretical research paper. However, several important issues remain before it is suitable for submission.

Main strengths

- Appropriate academic structure.
- Meaningful distinction between existing characterizations and new algorithmic contributions.
- Substantial mathematical development.
- Explicit complexity bounds and limitations.
- Good use of formal verification and exact arithmetic.
- Successful LaTeX compilation.

Main weaknesses
- An unresolved logical contradiction in the worked example.
- Inconsistent descriptions of chart enumeration.
- Some complexity-related proof obligations remain unaudited.
- Insufficiently reproducible presentation of the disputed example.
- Some unnecessarily emphatic language and repetitive explanations.

Overall recommendation
The paper is structurally and linguistically close to an acceptable academic manuscript, but its mathematical presentation and verification are not yet sufficiently consistent for submission.

I would not recommend a complete restructuring or extensive rewriting. The existing organization is fundamentally sound.

The most productive approach is a targeted revision focusing on mathematical consistency, proof completeness, and more precise presentation of the algorithmic contributions.

In particular, I would prioritize resolving the worked-example contradiction and auditing the complexity claims before spending substantial time on stylistic polishing.

Once these issues are addressed, the manuscript should be reassessed for submission readiness.

Suggested next step: Conduct a theorem-by-theorem mathematical audit of the revised manuscript, concentrating first on the fixed-parameter complexity theorem, the construction guarantees, and the worked example.
