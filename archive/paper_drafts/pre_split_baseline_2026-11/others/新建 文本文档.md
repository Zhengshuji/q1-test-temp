# **Comprehensive review of *Parameterized Algorithms for Functional Observer Feasibility***

I have examined the manuscript `q1_parametric_algorithms.tex` and compared its mathematical and algorithmic claims with the research developments and proof issues from our earlier discussions, particularly the November 10–12 revisions.

## **1. Overall assessment**

Major revision

Promising theoretical contribution

My assessment is that this manuscript has developed into a coherent and potentially publishable theoretical computer science paper. Its strongest contribution is not simply an algorithm for functional observer feasibility. It is the identification of a lower-dimensional representation of the free observer rows, followed by a finite algebraic characterization of the spectral condition that makes parameterized complexity analysis possible.

The central mathematical strategy is sound in its overall direction:

\\[ \boxed{ \text{Observer conditions} \longrightarrow \text{Compressed pencil} \longrightarrow \text{Effective subspace} \longrightarrow \text{Finite algebraic certificate} \longrightarrow \text{Parameterized algorithms}. } \\]

There are three particularly valuable aspects.

1\. **Structural compression:** The original state-space problem is reduced using the common kernel of \\(C\\) and \\(CA\\). This is the foundation of the paper and gives the subsequent complexity results their meaning.

2\. **Effective parameter:** The parameter\
\\[\
\ell=\operatorname{rank}(AN)\
\\]\
captures the dimension of the space in which the relevant free-row subspaces live. Unlike a parameter based directly on the number of free observer rows, it depends only on the system and fixed functionals.

3\. **Finite spectral certificate:** The conversion of an all-\\(\lambda\\) constant-rank condition into a finite polynomial condition, followed by a Cauchy–Binet compression, is the key algorithmic innovation.

However, I would distinguish three levels of mathematical maturity:

- The structural reduction is substantially developed.
- The algebraic certificate and its use in the decision algorithms require particularly careful completeness and soundness arguments.
- The bit-complexity and construction claims need a more systematic proof audit than the abstract algebraic argument alone.

The most important point is that the paper should not present an algebraic existence argument as a complete deterministic bit-complexity result without explicitly establishing the sizes and heights of every polynomial system involved.

My recommendation is to preserve the current central approach, rather than redesigning the paper. The remaining work is primarily about strengthening the proof architecture, tightening the algorithmic statements, and making the contribution easier to verify independently.

## **2. The mathematical core: detailed assessment**

### **2.1. Compression to the common kernel**

This is the strongest part of the manuscript.

The paper defines

\\[ K_0=\ker\begin{bmatrix}C\\\CA\end{bmatrix}, \qquad N\in\mathbb Q^{n\times k}, \\]

where the columns of \\(N\\) form a basis of \\(K_0\\), and introduces

\\[ H=LN,\qquad H_A=LAN. \\]

The rank identity

\\[ \operatorname{rank} \begin{bmatrix} P\\\M \end{bmatrix} = \operatorname{rank}P+\operatorname{rank}(MN_P) \\]

is then used to establish the equivalences

\\[ (5a) \iff \operatorname{row}H_A\subseteq\operatorname{row}H, \\]

and, under (5a),

\\[ (5b) \iff \operatorname{rank}(\lambda H-H_A) =\operatorname{rank}H, \quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb C. \\]

This is a very effective reduction.

**What is particularly good**

- The reduction preserves both observer conditions, not merely one of them.
- It correctly handles the complex spectral parameter.
- It separates the state-space dimension from the dimension of the compressed pencil.
- The proof of the rank identity is elementary and suitable for formal verification.
- The implication\
  \\[\
  \operatorname{rank}H_A=\operatorname{rank}H\
  \\]\
  is correctly obtained by evaluating the spectral condition at \\(\lambda=0\\).

I would promote the compression lemma to a central theorem, perhaps titled:

*The exact compressed characterization of functional observer feasibility.*

This theorem is the foundation on which every later result depends. Giving it greater prominence would improve the paper's mathematical presentation.

**One improvement: make the zero-rank convention explicit.**

The maximal-minor and gcd formulations require special treatment when \\(\omega=0\\). The manuscript mentions that the condition is vacuous, but this convention should be stated before introducing the polynomial certificate.

I recommend explicitly separating:

\\[ \omega=0: \quad H=0,\quad H_A=0 \\]

from the positive-rank case. This will prevent readers from interpreting the gcd of an empty collection of minors as an ordinary polynomial gcd.

### **2.2. The effective free-row dimension**

The paper introduces

\\[ \mathcal T=\operatorname{row}[N\ AN], \qquad d_0=\dim\mathcal T, \\]

and

\\[ \kappa\_{\mathrm{obs}}=rd_0. \\]

The subsequent reduction to

\\[ W=\operatorname{row}[H\ H_A] \\]

is mathematically important because it removes redundant representations of the same observer.

I particularly like the distinction between:

\\[ \kappa\_{\mathrm{obs}}=rd_0 \\]

and

\\[ \ell=\operatorname{rank}(AN). \\]

The first measures the number of coordinates required to represent the free rows. The second governs the dimension of the feasible subspace search.

This distinction should remain central to the paper.

The sandwich characterization

\\[ W_0\subseteq W\subseteq\mathcal T, \qquad \dim(W/W_0)\le r \\]

is also well motivated, and the converse realization argument is correct: every such subspace can be generated by adding preimages of a basis of \\(W/W_0\\).

#### **Important correction: the FPT algorithm needs an explicit feasibility check**

There is a missing condition in Algorithm 2.

The algorithm defines

\\[ m'=\dim\bigl(\mathcal T\cap(S\times S)\bigr)-s_0, \\]

where

\\[ S=\operatorname{row}(AN). \\]

However, the Grassmannian parameterization of \\(W/W_0\\) is valid only if

\\[ \boxed{ W_0\subseteq\mathcal T\cap(S\times S). } \\]

The fact that \\(W_0\subseteq\mathcal T\\) is automatic, but the additional inclusion

\\[ W_0\subseteq S\times S \\]

is not automatic.

A feasible \\(W\\) must satisfy this inclusion because the two projections of \\(W\\) coincide and are contained in \\(S\\). Consequently, it is a necessary preliminary feasibility condition.

**Required algorithmic correction:**

Before computing the quotient dimension or enumerating Grassmannian charts, perform the exact rational test

\\[ W_0\subseteq\mathcal T\cap(S\times S). \\]

If this fails, return infeasible.

If it holds, define

\\[ \mathcal V=\mathcal T\cap(S\times S), \qquad m'=\dim\mathcal V-\dim W_0. \\]

Then the relevant Grassmannian is indeed

\\[ \operatorname{Gr}(t,\mathcal V/W_0). \\]

This is a small modification to the algorithm but an important correction to its logical completeness.

I would also change the algorithm's range from

\\[ t=0,\ldots,2\ell \\]

to

\\[ t=0,\ldots,\min(r,2\ell). \\]

The larger range does not necessarily invalidate the decision procedure if all branches are otherwise well-defined, but the revised range is more precise and avoids searching for subspaces that cannot be realized with the prescribed number of free rows.

### **2.3. The structural bound**

The result

\\[ \boxed{\omega+1\le\dim W\le2\omega} \\]

for a feasible positive-rank subspace is a useful structural theorem.

The upper bound follows from

\\[ \pi_1(W)=\pi_2(W)=U \\]

and therefore

\\[ W\subseteq U\times U. \\]

The lower bound uses the fact that if \\(\dim W=\omega\\), then \\(W\\) is the graph of a linear operator \\(F:U\to U\\). Over \\(\mathbb C\\), that operator has an eigenvalue, contradicting the requirement that every \\(\pi\_\lambda(W)\\) have full dimension.

The argument is elegant and appropriately exploits the complex spectral condition.

I suggest making one detail more explicit: all projections involving complex \\(\lambda\\) must be understood on the complexification of the real subspace \\(W\\). The manuscript already mentions this, but it would be useful to repeat the convention at the beginning of the proof of the structural bound.

### **2.4. Monotonicity, saturation and minimum order**

The results

\\[ \mathrm{DEC}(q)\Longrightarrow\mathrm{DEC}(q+1) \\]

and

\\[ \mathrm{DEC}(r_0+r) = \mathrm{DEC}(r_0+m), \qquad r\ge m, \\]

are correct.

The minimum-order characterization

\\[ q\_{\min}=r_0+t\_{\min} \\]

with

\\[ t\_{\min}\le2\ell \\]

is a useful consequence.

One presentational improvement: distinguish carefully between the following three notions:

- Feasibility with at most \\(r\\) free rows.
- The minimum dimension of \\(W/W_0\\).
- The minimum number of rows needed to realize a particular \\(W\\).

The paper essentially handles these correctly, but using a single notation \\(t\\) for all three without always specifying which interpretation is intended can make the arguments harder to follow.

I recommend defining \\(t(W)=\dim(W/W_0)\\) once and using it consistently.

## **3. The finite spectral certificate: the central technical contribution**

This section deserves the most attention because it is where the structural reduction becomes an actual algorithm.

### **3.1. The polynomial left-inverse characterization**

For

\\[ P(\lambda)=\lambda H-H_A, \\]

the spectral condition is

\\[ \operatorname{rank}P(\lambda)=\omega \quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb C. \\]

The manuscript correctly identifies this with the condition that the maximal minors have no common complex root.

It then uses the equivalence

\\[ \operatorname{rank}P(\lambda)=\omega \quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb C \\]

if and only if there exists a polynomial left inverse

\\[ S(\lambda)P(\lambda)=I\_\omega. \\]

This is a particularly good choice of certificate.

The important advantage is that the universal quantifier over the complex spectral variable is replaced by a finite set of polynomial coefficient equations.

The degree estimate

\\[ \boxed{\deg S\le2\omega-2} \\]

is also correctly motivated by the adjugate–Bézout construction:

\\[ \sum_I g_I(\lambda)\det P_I(\lambda)=1, \\]

where

\\[ \deg g_I<\omega, \qquad \deg\operatorname{adj}(P_I)\le\omega-1. \\]

Therefore,

\\[ \deg S\le(\omega-1)+(\omega-1). \\]

This argument works for rectangular pencils because the individual selected submatrices \\(P_I\\) are square.

I consider this one of the paper's strongest technical results.

### **3.2. The Bézout degree-bound lemma**

The proof in Lemma 6 is more constructive than a bare appeal to the Euclidean algorithm.

It first chooses a linear combination

\\[ g=\sum_i a_if_i \\]

and then another combination

\\[ h=\sum_i b_if_i \\]

that avoids all roots of \\(g\\). This establishes coprimality and allows a two-polynomial Bézout identity.

The argument is valid, including the use of the fact that a finite union of proper real linear subspaces cannot cover the whole coefficient space.

However, I recommend addressing two technical points.

**First:** explicitly handle the case where \\(g\\) is a nonzero constant. In that case the conclusion is immediate, and there is no need to introduce its roots.

**Second:** state the degree-zero case separately. The proof uses bounds involving \\(\deg g\\) and \\(\deg h\\); readers should not have to infer the convention for zero or constant polynomials.

These are relatively minor issues, but this lemma is important enough to warrant a completely polished proof.

### **3.3. The Gram certificate**

The manuscript establishes

\\[ e_0^{\mathsf T}\in\operatorname{row}Z \\]

if and only if

\\[ \boxed{Z^{\mathsf T}Zx=e_0} \\]

has a real solution.

This is correct because

\\[ \operatorname{im}(Z^{\mathsf T}Z) = \operatorname{im}Z^{\mathsf T}. \\]

The proof through equality of kernels is straightforward.

I particularly appreciate that the manuscript explicitly explains why the degree window

\\[ \deg g_i<\omega \\]

is sufficient. This avoids a common gap in finite Bézout-certificate arguments: proving the existence of some polynomial representation without establishing that its degree fits the proposed finite encoding.

### **3.4. Cauchy–Binet compression**

This is arguably the most original algorithmic step.

The manuscript defines

\\[ K(\lambda,\mu) = \sum\_{|J|=\omega} \det\left( P(\lambda)\_{:,J}^{\mathsf T} P(\mu)\_{:,J} \right) \\]

and uses Cauchy–Binet to obtain

\\[ K(\lambda,\mu) = \sum\_{I,J} f\_{I,J}(\lambda)f\_{I,J}(\mu). \\]

This is the Gram kernel of all maximal minors.

The coefficient identity then gives

\\[ (Z^{\mathsf T}Z)\_{t,t'} = \sum\_{j=0}^{\omega-1} S\_{t-j,t'-j}. \\]

The conceptual achievement is important: the algorithm can recover the required Gram certificate without explicitly enumerating all maximal minors.

**There is, however, a notation and implementation issue that should be fixed.**

Initially,

\\[ P(\lambda)\in\mathbb R[\lambda]^{q\times k}. \\]

The Cauchy–Binet expression is written using column subsets of \\([k]\\), but the claimed complexity is

\\[ \binom{\ell}{\omega}\le2^\ell. \\]

That bound is justified only after restricting the column coordinates to

\\[ S=\operatorname{row}(AN), \qquad \dim S=\ell, \\]

and applying the corresponding rational coordinate transformation.

The paper should explicitly define the transformed pencil

\\[ \widetilde P(\lambda)=P(\lambda)Y_S \\]

and explain that its last \\(k-\ell\\) columns vanish. The Cauchy–Binet sum can then be restricted to the first \\(\ell\\) columns.

Without this explicit transition, the expression and its complexity bound appear inconsistent.

This is a presentation issue with consequences for the proof of the FPT theorem; it should not be left implicit.

## **4. The fixed-order algorithm**

Theorem 1 claims

\\[ T\_{\mathrm{DEC}(q)} = \mathcal L^{O(q^2)}. \\]

This is an important result because it establishes polynomial-time decidability for every fixed observer order, independently of the state dimension in the exponent.

### **4.1. What works**

The pivot factorization

\\[ H=XB,\qquad B\_{:,J}=I\_\omega \\]

is an appropriate way to eliminate the nonlinear rank constraint on \\(H\\).

The use of

\\[ e\_\omega(X^{\mathsf T}X)>0 \\]

to impose full column rank is also correct over the reals.

The consistency condition

\\[ Mb=c \\]

is converted into

\\[ \operatorname{rank}(MM^{\mathsf T}) = \operatorname{rank}(MM^{\mathsf T}+cc^{\mathsf T}). \\]

This is a good algebraic encoding because both matrices are positive semidefinite and their ranks can be characterized using elementary symmetric polynomials.

The soundness and completeness argument in Theorem 1 is reasonably clear.

In particular, the reconstruction

\\[ H=XB,\qquad H_A=GB \\]

together with a left inverse \\(U\\) of \\(X\\) gives

\\[ H_A=GU\\,H. \\]

Thus the algebraic observer condition is recovered.

### **4.2. Improvements needed**

**A. Separate the zero-rank branch explicitly.**

The algorithm currently starts with \\(\omega=0\\), although the subsequent certificate machinery is developed for \\(\omega\ge1\\).

I recommend:

- Handle \\(\omega=0\\) using the direct test \\(H=H_A=0\\).
- Apply pivot factorization, rank consistency and the Gram certificate only for \\(\omega\ge1\\).

This makes the implementation unambiguous.

**B. Give the linear system&#x20;**\\(M(X,G)b=c(X,G)\\)**&#x20;explicitly.**

At present, the paper explains how to obtain it but leaves the actual coefficient structure implicit.

For a theoretical algorithm paper, this is not ideal. The reader should be able to reconstruct the system without reverse-engineering the prose.

I recommend adding a compact proposition specifying:

1. The unknown entries of \\(B\\) after fixing \\(B\_{:,J}=I\_\omega\\).
2. The fixed-row equations.
3. The equations enforcing membership in \\(\mathcal T\\).
4. The vectorization convention.
5. The resulting dimensions of \\(M\\) and \\(c\\).

This would also make the polynomial-size claim easier to audit.

**C. Clarify the number of charts.**

The estimate

\\[ \sum\_{\omega=0}^{q}\binom{k}{\omega} \le (q+1)k^q \\]

is sufficient for the fixed-\\(q\\) analysis, but the endpoint cases should be treated separately, especially \\(k=0\\) and \\(\omega=0\\).

The proof should state the bound for \\(k\ge1\\), rather than silently applying it to \\(k=0\\).

**D. Improve the theorem's presentation.**

I suggest separating the correctness proof from the complexity proof:

- Part I: equivalence between feasibility and the finite polynomial system.
- Part II: bounds on variables, equations, degrees and coefficient bit lengths.
- Part III: application of Renegar's algorithm.

This separation will make the proof considerably easier to assess.

## **5. The fixed-parameter algorithm: the most important remaining issue**

Theorem 2 claims

\\[ \boxed{ T\_{\mathrm{DEC}} \le \exp\bigl(O(\ell^2\log(\ell+2))\bigr) \operatorname{poly}(\mathcal L), } \\]

where

\\[ \ell=\operatorname{rank}(AN). \\]

This is potentially the most significant result of the paper.

It is stronger conceptually than simply obtaining an FPT algorithm in the number of free observer coordinates. The parameter does not depend on \\(r\\).

However, I found two important problems in the current presentation.

### **5.1. The preprocessing complexity contains the wrong parameter**

In the discussion following Theorem 2, the manuscript states that computing the Cauchy–Binet kernel requires at most

\\[ \binom{k}{\omega}\le 2^{\kappa\_{\mathrm{obs}}} \\]

determinants.

This does not establish the claimed FPT bound in \\(\ell\\).

The issue is that

\\[ \kappa\_{\mathrm{obs}}=rd_0 \\]

can grow with the number of free rows, even when \\(\ell\\) remains bounded.

The claimed theorem requires the preprocessing to be bounded by

\\[ f(\ell)\operatorname{poly}(\mathcal L), \\]

not by a function of \\(\kappa\_{\mathrm{obs}}\\).

The correction is available from the preceding structural reduction.

After changing coordinates so that the row space

\\[ S=\operatorname{row}(AN) \\]

occupies the first \\(\ell\\) coordinates, every feasible pencil has only \\(\ell\\) potentially nonzero columns.

Consequently, the Cauchy–Binet computation should use

\\[ \boxed{ \binom{\ell}{\omega}\le2^\ell } \\]

determinants, not \\(\binom{k}{\omega}\\).

The coefficient calculation and the subsequent bit-complexity analysis should consistently use this reduced representation.

**This correction is essential.** As written, the preprocessing paragraph does not establish the claimed dependence solely on \\(\ell\\).

### **5.2. The FPT Grassmannian parameterization needs a more explicit construction**

The paper uses

\\[ \operatorname{Gr}(t,m') \\]

with

\\[ m'\le2\ell-s_0. \\]

This is the right general direction.

However, the implementation of the chart coordinates needs to be more precise.

I recommend introducing the following objects explicitly:

\\[ \mathcal V=\mathcal T\cap(S\times S), \\]

\\[ W_0\subseteq\mathcal V, \\]

\\[ m'=\dim\mathcal V-\dim W_0. \\]

Choose a rational basis of \\(W_0\\), extend it to a rational basis of \\(\mathcal V\\), and then express every candidate subspace as

\\[ W=W_0+\operatorname{row}(Y\_{\mathrm{chart}}). \\]

For each Grassmannian chart, the resulting matrix should have dimensions

\\[ \nu\times 2\ell, \qquad \nu=s_0+t. \\]

The first and second blocks then provide the matrices used in the algebraic and spectral tests.

This explicit construction would address several issues simultaneously:

- It makes the chart dimension transparent.
- It establishes that every admissible \\(W\\) is represented.
- It makes the polynomial dependence of the matrix entries clear.
- It simplifies the complexity analysis.

I would also use different notation for the chart matrix, its two projection blocks, and the Gram-certificate matrix. The current reuse of \\(B\\), \\(G\\), and related symbols makes this section unnecessarily difficult to read.

### **5.3. The structural bound should be integrated into the algorithm's correctness proof**

The manuscript correctly establishes

\\[ \omega+1\le\dim W\le2\omega \\]

for positive-rank feasible subspaces.

But the algorithm should explain explicitly how the bounds determine the finite search.

For every feasible \\(W\\),

\\[ \omega\le\ell, \\]

and therefore

\\[ \dim W\le2\ell. \\]

This implies

\\[ t=\dim(W/W_0)\le2\ell. \\]

The algorithm should make clear that this is not merely a bound on the number of variables: it is the reason that only finitely many quotient dimensions need to be examined.

The proof of completeness should then proceed in this order:

1. Every feasible observer produces a feasible subspace \\(W\\).
2. The structural theorem gives \\(t\le2\ell\\).
3. The containment check guarantees \\(W_0\subseteq\mathcal V\\).
4. Every such quotient subspace belongs to one of the enumerated Grassmannian charts.
5. The polynomial conditions characterize exactly the two observer conditions.
6. Therefore, the algorithm accepts if and only if a feasible observer exists.

This would make the FPT theorem substantially more convincing.

### **5.4. The parameterized complexity bound**

Once the preceding issues are corrected, the intended variable count is

\\[ N=t(m'-t)+2\omega. \\]

Using

\\[ m'\le2\ell, \qquad \omega\le\ell, \\]

gives

\\[ N\le\ell^2+2\ell. \\]

The number of charts is bounded by

\\[ \sum\_{t=0}^{m'}\binom{m'}{t} =2^{m'} \le4^\ell. \\]

If the polynomial system has

\\[ s=O(\ell),\qquad D=O(\ell), \\]

and coefficient bit length

\\[ B=f(\ell)\operatorname{poly}(\mathcal L), \\]

then Renegar's bound yields

\\[ B(\log B)(\log\log B)(sD)^{O(N)} = \exp\bigl(O(\ell^2\log(\ell+2))\bigr) \operatorname{poly}(\mathcal L). \\]

The asymptotic conclusion is therefore consistent with the intended construction.

My recommendation is to retain this theorem, but not to regard its current proof as fully closed until the preprocessing parameter mismatch and the Grassmannian construction have been corrected.

## **6. Bit complexity: a proof audit is still necessary**

This is the second major area requiring attention.

The paper is appropriately careful to distinguish arithmetic complexity from bit complexity. That distinction is important and should be preserved.

### **6.1. The coefficient-size lemma**

The intended chain is:

\\[ (A,C,L_0) \longrightarrow N \longrightarrow H,H_A \longrightarrow \text{polynomial minors} \longrightarrow Z^{\mathsf T}Z \longrightarrow \text{real-algebraic system}. \\]

The manuscript attempts to bound the bit length at every stage using denominator clearing and Hadamard's inequality.

This is the right strategy.

However, the proof currently contains an unjustified dimensional assumption.

In Section 6, it states

\\[ n,p,q\le n\le\mathcal L. \\]

The inequality \\(p\le n\\) does not follow from the problem formulation: the output matrix \\(C\\) can have more rows than columns.

Likewise,

\\[ m=n^2+pn+r_0n\le3n^2 \\]

is not generally justified.

There are two ways to fix this.

**Preferred option:** Preprocess \\(C\\) by replacing it with a rational basis of its row space. This preserves

\\[ \operatorname{row}C \\]

and

\\[ \operatorname{row}\begin{bmatrix}C\\\CA\end{bmatrix}, \\]

so it preserves the observer conditions and the common kernel. The resulting output matrix has at most \\(n\\) rows.

Then state explicitly that this preprocessing is performed before the complexity analysis.

**Alternative:** Retain the original \\(p\\) and replace the estimate with

\\[ m=O(n^2+pn+r_0n), \\]

then express the final bounds in terms of \\(\mathcal L\\), using the fact that all dimensions are bounded by the input length.

Either approach works. The first gives cleaner notation.

### **6.2. The rational basis construction needs a more detailed argument**

The proof states that \\(N\\), the basis of \\(\mathcal T\\), and \\(D\\) can be obtained by Cramer's rule or reduced row echelon form.

This is plausible, but the argument is too compressed.

In particular, the bit size of \\(D\\) is not automatically covered by the determinant bound on the original input matrix: \\(D\\) is constructed from the rational matrix \\([N\ AN]\\), whose entries already have nontrivial denominators.

I recommend proving a separate lemma:

**Lemma (Rational basis and annihilator bit bounds).**

For a rational matrix \\(M\\) with input bit length \\(B\\), a basis of its row space, a basis of its nullspace, and a rational matrix defining the annihilator of its row space can all be computed in polynomial bit complexity, with polynomially bounded output bit length.

Then apply this result successively to

\\[ [C;CA] \\]

and

\\[ [N\ AN]. \\]

This avoids an unnecessarily delicate direct denominator argument.

### **6.3. The Gram coefficient bounds**

The bounds on the coefficients of the minors are reasonable.

The compression step should then establish that the coefficients of

\\[ K(\lambda,\mu) \\]

and the entries of \\(Z^{\mathsf T}Z\\) have bit length bounded by

\\[ f(\ell)\operatorname{poly}(\mathcal L). \\]

The paper should explicitly distinguish:

- The number of summands.
- The bit length of an individual summand.
- The bit length after summation.
- The cost of actually constructing the coefficients.

The inequality

\\[ \operatorname{bits}\left(\sum\_{i=1}^T a_i\right) \le b+O(\log T) \\]

when each summand has bit length at most \\(b\\) is useful, but it does not by itself establish the cost of computing all summands.

That computational cost must be included in the preprocessing analysis.

### **6.4. Renegar's theorem**

The manuscript uses the bound

\\[ B(\log B)(\log\log B)(sD)^{O(N)}. \\]

This is an appropriate type of bound for the intended result, but I recommend checking the precise formulation and hypotheses against the cited version of Renegar's theorem.

In particular, the paper should specify:

- Whether coefficients are integer or rational.
- The normalization used when clearing denominators.
- Whether \\(s\\) counts equations and inequalities separately.
- How strict inequalities are represented.
- Whether the stated bound includes construction of the polynomial system.

The current explanation is close, but a theorem statement with explicit hypotheses would be better.

## **7. The zero-free-row case: a valuable independent result**

The exact polynomial-time test for \\(r=0\\) is a good addition.

The manuscript establishes the equivalence

\\[ \operatorname{rank}(\lambda X-G)=\omega \quad\forall\lambda\in\mathbb C \\]

if and only if there exists a polynomial matrix \\(S\\) satisfying

\\[ S(\lambda)(\lambda X-G)=I\_\omega \\]

with

\\[ \deg S\le2\omega-2. \\]

Writing

\\[ S(\lambda)=\sum\_{j=0}^{2\omega-2}S_j\lambda^j \\]

produces the finite rational linear system

\\[ -S_0G=I\_\omega, \\]

\\[ S\_{j-1}X-S_jG=0, \qquad 1\le j\le2\omega-2, \\]

\\[ S\_{2\omega-2}X=0. \\]

This is an elegant way to avoid explicit enumeration of maximal minors.

The distinction between the existence proof and the algorithm is especially important:

- The adjugate–Bézout argument proves that a certificate of bounded degree exists.
- The algorithm finds the certificate by solving a polynomial-size linear system.

This resolves the earlier concern that enumerating all maximal minors would make the \\(r=0\\) algorithm exponential.

### **7.1. Remaining complexity issue**

The manuscript gives the number of variables and equations:

\\[ N\_{\mathrm{var}}=(2\omega-1)q\omega, \\]

\\[ N\_{\mathrm{eq}}=2\omega^3. \\]

These are polynomial.

However, the bit-complexity argument should be made more rigorous.

The current estimate uses a generic Hadamard bound and then multiplies it by a cubic arithmetic-operation count. That is not quite a complete specification of a fraction-free elimination algorithm.

I recommend stating the exact linear algebra procedure and citing a standard polynomial-time rational linear-system algorithm, including its bit-complexity bound.

For example, one can use fraction-free Gaussian elimination with an explicit bound on intermediate integer sizes, or a modular algorithm with rational reconstruction.

The proposition itself is important and should be retained. I would not remove it merely because its implementation proof needs tightening.

### **7.2. The Kronecker canonical form remark**

The interpretation of the pencil condition is useful:

- Right singular blocks are excluded.
- Finite regular elementary-divisor blocks are excluded.
- Left singular blocks are allowed.
- Infinite elementary divisors are allowed.

This correctly emphasizes that the condition concerns full column rank at every finite complex spectral value, not full rank at infinity.

I would retain this remark, perhaps moving it to the discussion following the polynomial left-inverse theorem.

## **8. Existential-real membership**

Theorem 3 establishes

\\[ \mathrm{DEC}(q)\in\exists\mathbb R \\]

and

\\[ \mathrm{MINDEC}(Q)\in\exists\mathbb R. \\]

The appendix is one of the better-organized parts of the manuscript.

Its main idea is to introduce

\\[ H=XB,\qquad H_A=GB \\]

and impose

\\[ UX=I\_\omega,\qquad BV=I\_\omega. \\]

The first identity ensures that \\(X\\) has full column rank and permits reconstruction of the algebraic relation. The second ensures that \\(B\\) has full row rank and preserves the rank of the pencil.

The polynomial identity

\\[ S(\lambda)(\lambda X-G)=I\_\omega \\]

then gives a finite algebraic representation of the spectral condition.

The constraints

\\[ D \begin{bmatrix} (X_iB)^{\mathsf T}\\\ (G_iB)^{\mathsf T} \end{bmatrix} =0 \\]

are also essential. They ensure that the free-row pairs are actually realizable by rows of the original observer matrix.

This last point is important: without these constraints, the existential formula would characterize arbitrary compressed matrix pairs rather than observer feasibility.

### **8.1. Soundness and completeness**

The appendix correctly separates the two directions.

**Soundness:** A solution to the polynomial system produces an observer \\(L\\), satisfies the algebraic condition, and preserves full column rank of the pencil at every complex \\(\lambda\\).

**Completeness:** A feasible observer supplies a rank factorization, a polynomial left inverse, and solutions to all the encoding constraints.

This is the right proof structure.

I recommend making the following improvements.

1. Give the \\(\omega=0\\) branch its own explicitly defined formula, rather than introducing generic left-inverse coefficients with a negative degree range.
2. Provide a complete table of the numbers of variables, equations, and maximum degrees.
3. Explain the rational coefficient encoding and denominator clearing in the appendix itself.
4. State explicitly that the formula is constructed in polynomial time from the input.

There is also an opportunity to strengthen the result.

The appendix appears to construct a polynomial-size existential formula even when \\(q\\) is part of the input, subject to the stated restriction \\(q\le n\\). The current theorem only emphasizes fixed \\(q\\).

I suggest checking the encoding-size argument carefully and, if it holds without an additional fixed-parameter assumption, stating the stronger result:

\\[ \boxed{\mathrm{DEC}\in\exists\mathbb R} \\]

for the unrestricted prescribed-order decision problem.

The current finite threshold result would then follow directly.

This is a worthwhile strengthening because membership in \\(\exists\mathbb R\\) is a natural complexity classification independent of the two parameterized algorithms.

### **9.3. Explain the significance of the parameter**

The statement that \\(\ell\\) does not depend on \\(r\\) is mathematically correct, but readers should be told why this matters.

The key observation is that increasing the number of available observer rows does not increase the dimension of the effective spectral search space.

In other words, the number of available free rows determines how large a subspace can be realized, but the system itself determines the ambient space in which that subspace must lie.

This is a strong conceptual interpretation and deserves a short paragraph in the introduction.

The conclusion appropriately acknowledges the large parameter-dependent factor.

### **14.1. The most important strategic recommendation**

I would make the following result the central theorem of the paper:

\\[ \boxed{ \mathrm{DEC} \text{ is FPT parameterized by } \ell=\operatorname{rank}(AN). } \\]

This is the result that distinguishes the manuscript from a conventional algebraic reformulation of functional observer feasibility.
